Read the rhythm

Every burst represents one selected model. The horizontal axis is the calendar, moving at constant speed; each rocket climbs straight up from its release date and bursts at the launched model's Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI) score, so the bursts trace capability rising over time. The camera never shakes or moves to the beat: it glides with the calendar and the newest scores, tightens slowly in stages as the music builds, and pulls back once to the whole chart at the observation cutoff. A step line per lab follows its best score so far. Beside the chart (under it in portrait), a launch-pace line works like an indicator with a price chart: an exponential moving average of models (60-day time constant), annualized to MODELS / YEAR and kicked up by every model. Its gridlines mark one model every 60, 30, 14 and 7 days, plus 3 days once the peak substantially exceeds the weekly cadence and the gridline fits, and its scale widens whenever the pace sets a new high. Launches that Epoch AI has not scored spray a low fountain on the date axis and show “—” instead of a number. The first model of each announcement bursts at the release date; its other models follow at sixteenth-note intervals. Positions stay at each model’s date and ECI; trajectories begin 0.36 seconds before each burst. Each lab keeps its own burst form and marker shape: OpenAI, peony bursts and circles; Anthropic, glitter bursts and diamonds; Google, double-ring bursts and squares; DeepSeek, fan bursts and triangles; Meta, four-point star bursts and crosses. Where bursts crowd together only their glow and trails dim; each keeps its full size, rays and lifetime. Each tag row names one announcement: all its models in ECI order, with shared leading words elided and every score shown (— if unscored). Announcements a moment apart share one tag of at most three rows; additional announcements are counted towards the lists. Each lab’s list shows up to four newest announcements, newest on top, with all model names, the date and the leading model’s score. Only names too long at the minimum size wrap to two lines; joined names break after a separator, and dates sit directly below the last name line. New arrivals push by their actual height; older rows fade at the column edge. Every announcement is fully inside its column for at least 1.4 seconds. Each model keeps its own preview asterisk and marker; a callout glyph is hollow only when all its models are previews.

Selection

One differently named main-family general-purpose model is one burst, including named small/fast tiers such as Haiku, mini, nano, Instant, Flash, Flash-Lite, Lite and Glimmer. Google includes PaLM 2 before Gemini. Parameter-count-only sizes share one record. Same-name updates, added availability/input/context, hidden routing/fallback variants, compute-only modes, specialists/media/safety, distillations/research/device-only models, auxiliary open-weight families and restricted/unreleased models are excluded; see the full methodology below.

Capability scores

Burst heights use the Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI) by Epoch AI, licensed CC BY 4.0, retrieved 2026-10-19 (CSV). 100 of 116 selected models have a score. ECI combines many benchmarks onto one scale, and Epoch publishes an uncertainty interval several points wide for each model, so small gaps are not rankings. Each record has its own score; same-day models have separate bursts. This visualization is not affiliated with or endorsed by Epoch AI.
