# Reference observation

Reference: [Moniiii / @miniii_codes, X, September 27, 2026](https://x.com/miniii_codes/status/2103938407558947265). Inspected on 2026-10-02 in a dedicated browser tab.

The public post text and embedded media were visible. The embedded player reported approximately 29.1 seconds. We inspected the title frame and a later chart frame around 23 seconds. The video uses a dark portrait composition with a horizontal calendar, green and warm orange stems on opposite sides, and OpenAI / Anthropic labels. The density of event marks increases toward the right. Small individual model labels could not be reliably read at the embedded display size. X's fullscreen link led to a sign-in prompt, so fullscreen content and an exact frame-by-frame reproduction were not verified.

The post discusses approximate rates of one major release per 73 days in 2023 and per 18 days in 2026, and explicitly notes that the result depends on what counts as major. Those estimates motivated investigating a sourced release calendar; they were not imported as data.

This implementation independently creates the typography, night-sky background, analytical fireworks, newest-first model-name lists, year views, cursor, summary graphics, original synthesized soundtrack, captions and player page. It adds source-linked editable records, consistent year scales, preview markers, a partial-year cutoff, and explicit density math. No source video, screenshots, audio, provider logos or other copied creative assets are redistributed.

The second edition responds to the request for a fireworks-like rhythm and original BGM. It preserves the date/source records while replacing the stop-and-go clock with continuous calendar time. It does not copy the reference soundtrack or claim an exact visual match.

The third edition shortens the film to 30 seconds, replaces the score with a faster original 128 BPM track, and adds Google's Gemini line as a third lab with its own altitude, burst shape and color. These changes follow the project owner's request for a tighter, more energetic cut; they are not an attempt to match the reference video's length or soundtrack.

A follow-up revision replaces that track with “Liftoff / 003”, a score that builds across the calendar and drops at the observation cutoff, and orders each lab's model list newest first, with arrivals pushing older names down. Both changes follow the project owner's feedback on the third edition.

A further revision turns the stage into a date × capability chart at the project owner's suggestion: each burst opens at the launched model's score on the Epoch Capabilities Index (Epoch AI, CC BY 4.0), and per-lab step lines show capability rising. The owner's example was the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. It was not used because Artificial Analysis's website terms prohibit copying or republishing site content and its free API requires an account key. ECI is openly licensed and scores models from the whole 2023–2026 window on one scale.
